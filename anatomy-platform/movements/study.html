How to use this guide

You can complete the following practice tasks using this page alone. No video, model, color recognition or timed response is required.

  1. Identify which segment moves and which segment is held fixed in a demonstration.
  2. Describe the direction of the outward movement and the return movement.
  3. Distinguish a one-way hip video from an out-and-back animation.
  4. Recognize what a static comparison does and does not demonstrate.

Right and left refer to the model's anatomical sides, not the side of your screen. A source-rest pose is the starting pose stored in the source model; it is not a claim of an individual's neutral posture. Read each sequence in numbered order, then use the practice questions below.

Elbow flexion and extension

  1. Source start: upper arm fixed; forearm and hand extended.
  2. Flexion: forearm and hand bend toward the upper arm.
  3. Peak flexion: illustrative 120° offset.
  4. Extension: return toward source start.

Forearm rotation — revised axis

  1. Elbow held at 90°; ulna fixed.
  2. Radius and hand rotate together about the estimated forearm axis.
  3. Rotation endpoint: illustrative 140° excursion; pronation direction requires review.
  4. Radius and hand return along the same path.

Ankle dorsiflexion and plantarflexion

  1. Source-rest lower leg and foot.
  2. Dorsiflexion: foot moves toward the front of the lower leg; illustrative 20° offset.
  3. Return to source rest.
  4. Plantarflexion: foot points downward; illustrative 35° offset, then returns.

Hip flexion and extension

  1. Source-rest pelvis and right leg.
  2. Flexion: right leg moves forward; illustrative 90° offset.
  3. Return to source rest.
  4. Extension: right leg moves backward; illustrative 20° offset, then returns.

Hip abduction and adduction

  1. Source-rest pelvis and right leg at 0 seconds.
  2. Abduction: right leg moves away from the midline to an illustrative 40° offset at 1.5 seconds; held to 2 seconds.
  3. Adduction: right leg returns toward the midline between 2 and 3.5 seconds.
  4. Source-rest pose held to 4 seconds; leg does not cross the midline.

Hip flexion — video alternative

  1. 0 seconds: source-rest right leg, pelvis fixed and knee extended.
  2. 0–2.5 seconds: leg moves forward into hip flexion, reaching an illustrative 90° offset.
  3. 2.5–4 seconds: flexion endpoint held. No extension phase is shown.
  4. The video ends at the flexion endpoint.

Hip extension — video alternative

  1. 0 seconds: source-rest right leg, pelvis fixed and knee extended.
  2. 0–2.5 seconds: leg moves backward into hip extension, reaching an illustrative 20° offset.
  3. 2.5–4 seconds: extension endpoint held. No flexion phase is shown.
  4. The video ends at the extension endpoint.

Knee — original hinge study

  1. Source-rest extended knee; femur fixed.
  2. Lower leg and foot bend behind the thigh in flexion.
  3. Illustrative hinge endpoint: 120° offset.
  4. Lower leg and foot return toward extension.

  3. Endpoint: approximately 117.5° of lower-leg rotation.
  4. Lower leg and foot return toward the source-rest pose. Patella is not included.

In the elbow demonstration, what stays fixed and what moves?

The upper arm stays fixed. The forearm and hand bend toward the upper arm during flexion, then return during extension. The wrist and fingers remain fixed relative to the moving segment.

Shoulder abduction and return

The right humerus moves away from the trunk and returns. The scapula and clavicle are fixed in this illustrative joint-motion model. Use the source muscle descriptions to relate abduction to deltoid and supraspinatus.

  1. At 0 seconds, the arm is in its original source-rest pose, already slightly away from the trunk.
  2. From 0 to 1.5 seconds, the humerus moves farther outward. Forearm and hand follow as a rigid segment.
  3. From 1.5 to 2 seconds, hold at an additional 45-degree offset.
  4. From 2 to 3.5 seconds, return toward the source-rest pose. Hold until 4 seconds.
Which structures move and which remain fixed?

The humerus, forearm, and hand move together. Scapula, clavicle, sternum, and manubrium stay fixed. Elbow, wrist, and finger positions stay fixed relative to the arm.

Which two course muscles connect to this abduction activity?

Deltoid and supraspinatus. Review their attachments, actions, and innervation in the course lesson. This animation depicts bone motion; muscle activation and deformation need separate modeling.

How does this study simplify shoulder movement?

The study rotates the arm about a source rig pivot. Scapular movement, humeral axial rotation, joint translation, and muscle deformation remain to be modeled. The 45-degree offset is illustrative and requires instructor review.
